About

I came into data science through a B.Sc. in Statistics, which means my instinct on any model is to ask what the confidence interval looks like before I ask what the accuracy is. Most of my projects sit at that intersection — deep learning and big data pipelines built on top of hypothesis testing, goodness-of-fit, and a healthy suspicion of numbers that look too clean.

Recent work spans a BERT-based cognitive load predictor, a Hadoop/Spark pipeline processing 500,000+ Wikipedia edits, and a statistical audit of five state assembly elections using Benford's Law — the kind of project that only makes sense if you actually understand why the naive version of the test is wrong.

Currently looking for Data Scientist / Data Analyst internship roles where "let's check that against a null hypothesis" is a normal thing to say in a meeting.

Benford's Law Election Audit

5
STATES AUDITED

A statistical auditing pipeline applying Benford's Law to candidate-level vote totals across five Indian state assembly elections (Assam, Kerala, Puducherry, Tamil Nadu, West Bengal). Compared first- vs. second-digit conformity using Chi-square goodness-of-fit and Mean Absolute Deviation tests — showing first-digit tests are structurally invalid at the constituency level (turnout caps distort the leading digit), while second-digit tests confirmed statistical regularity across all five states, in line with peer-reviewed forensic methodology (Mebane, 2006).
